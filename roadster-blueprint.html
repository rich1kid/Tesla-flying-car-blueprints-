Tesla Roadster
Thrust & Suction Systems

Schematic · not to scale · not a Tesla drawing

Tesla showed its next Roadster at a SpaceX test site in Texas on 15 October 2026. It drove up a ramp and hung upside down, flipped in the air, leapt over a row of Teslas and landed on jets of air. These sheets explain the physics behind each trick. Every figure is either from a named report or marked as my own estimate.

Four corrections to the common version of the story

  • No jet engine and no fuel. The thrusters use compressed air from tanks. Nothing burns.
  • It hops. It does not fly. The demo car had no driver and was not shown in sustained flight.
  • The upside-down grip is suction fans. It is not a suspension feature.
  • The Chinese tunnel loop is a different trick. It relies on speed and a rigid chassis, and a Mercedes SLS AMG did a similar loop earlier.

Compressed-air thrusters

How it works

Air at very high pressure is released through a nozzle. The air rushes one way and pushes the car the other way. That is Newton's third law, the same principle SpaceX uses for small attitude jets. Nothing is burned, so there is no flame and no fuel tank.

What the numbers imply (my estimate)

  • 250 kg in 600 L is about 417 kg per cubic metre, roughly 340 times denser than air at sea level.
  • That is about 350–450 bar by the ideal gas law, with real-gas effects pushing it higher. Musk's 2018 plan quoted 10,000 psi (690 bar).
  • Reports say 4 nozzles are under the car and 2 at the front. The positions of all 10 were not published.

Staying level in the air

The problem

A car held up on jets has nothing like wings or wheels to keep it steady. If one jet pushes harder than the others, the car turns around its centre of mass. Left alone, a small tilt grows into a flip. Pushing at a distance r from the centre makes a turning force τ = r × F.

The fix

Sensors measure the tilt, a computer works out which nozzles to open and by how much, and the nozzles correct it. This loop runs again and again, many times a second. Rocket landings work the same way. In the demo, reports say two front thrusters helped turn the car right side up. Tesla has not published its control design, so this loop shows the standard method, not Tesla's actual code.

Fans that pin the car down

How the grip works

Skirts seal a space under the car. Fans pull air out of that space, so the pressure there falls below the pressure outside. The outside air then pushes the car toward the road with a force of ΔP × A. For 2 g on a 2-tonne car, that is 39 kN. Over 2–4 m² of floor, the pressure drop is only about 0.1–0.2 of an atmosphere.

History and limits

Race engineers have used the same idea since the Chaparral 2J (1970) and the Brabham BT46B (1978). Reports compare the Roadster to the McMurtry fan car. Tesla says tyres alone give about 1.26 g of grip, and the fans let it corner above 2 g. Upside down, suction must exceed the car's own weight before the tyres press on the surface at all.

Why it hops and does not cruise

Reading the chart

Holding 2 tonnes in the air needs about 20 kN of thrust. A real rotor moves a lot of air slowly, which is cheap in power. A jet of air at 500–700 m/s is the opposite, so it needs about 5.8 MW, more than six times what a 4 m² rotor needs. That is the reason helicopters and eVTOLs use big rotors.

Refilling the tank

The pump takes its power from the battery. Squeezing 250 kg of air to about 360 bar takes at least 35 kWh even with perfect cooling, and real compressors need more. The speed ceiling for room-temperature air is about 780 m/s, so even a perfect nozzle cannot hover for much longer than ten seconds.

The Chinese tunnel loop

What was reported

On 13 August 2026 Voyah said its Zhui Guang S became the first unmodified production car to complete a 360° loop in a closed test tunnel in Wuhan. It entered at about 130 km/h. The car has an aluminium double-wishbone front and five-link rear suspension, ten aero ducts, and 475 kW from two motors. The report says the Mercedes SLS AMG, a lighter supercar, had done a similar loop before.

What it is not

The loop does not use suction. At speed the car stays on the wall because its path is curved, the same reason water stays in a swung bucket. Tyres and suspension then have to take the load and hold the car's line. It is a real engineering result, but it is separate from Tesla's fans and does not show that Mercedes failed at anything.

What is still unknown

  • Battery capacity, power output and weight. Range figures conflict between outlets.
  • How long the car can stay airborne. The demo showed short hops with no driver.
  • Whether a road-legal car can carry the tanks and still meet crash rules. Edmunds noted the demo car bounced as if it had no suspension.
  • Whether 2028 production happens. A Smithsonian curator told CNN a hybrid would be a poor aircraft and a poor car.
